The browser is the prototype, the screen is the hardware. The seed-driven code is the work.

I make generative systems in code. For the past five years, that work has lived in the browser, and it will continue to — but browser-based work is inherently unstable as a long-term artifact. Browsers update on a yearly cycle, and work that runs today breaks tomorrow on a different browser or a later generation.

So the browser work becomes what it has quietly been all along: prototyping. It is where compositions are developed and tested.

The Physical Work

Each piece is a self-contained unit — a 4K screen with a small fanless computer mounted on the back. No internet, no Wi-Fi. Bluetooth only, for smartphone interactivity. The computer runs real-time generative animation. The current pieces are built in p5.js and three.js and are moving toward a native pipeline — OpenFrameworks, Pure Data, OSC, DSP. The unit hangs on the wall and plugs in. Seed-driven plotter output drawings may follow later. That is the whole work: screen, computer, code, sealed together.

Current Pieces
Centered Witness Mirror Surrender Machines Invisible Layer

Centered, Witness Mirror, Surrender Machines, and Invisible Layer exist now as proofs of concept. They establish the form and let me see which compositions hold at physical scale and which resonate with people. Work that resonates moves forward into editions.

Scale

The primary format is large — 43″ 4K, portrait and 55″ 4K, portrait. I am also testing a 27″ format as a distinct, more intimate object. Each size is treated as its own work; a piece at one scale is not a smaller copy of the piece at another; each work has its own seed and Machine DNA (genome and lifespan).

Distribution & Installation

The physical work is placed through art consultants. Installation is handled by local AV integrators — the unit is self-contained, so hanging it is straightforward. A service arrangement may travel with each piece: if the hardware fails, the integrator receives a freshly flashed replacement. The code is backed up, and the build is preserved, so a failed screen or computer is simply replaced and the work continues. The hardware is serviceable and replaceable; the work is the certified iteration of the code, not the machine that runs it.

Provenance

Provenance is recorded through Transient Labs' TRACE. The authenticated iteration of the code — its seed, edition, and certificate — is the work, not the hardware it runs on. A unit broken apart for its components is no longer the authenticated work; that is the owner's choice, and the record defines it as such.

What Lasts

The source is preserved, and the work is designed to be re-instantiated on replacement hardware over time. The intent is continuity of the work itself, independent of any single screen or machine.

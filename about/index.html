About Mark Walhimer

Mark Walhimer (b. 1964) is a generative artist and digital installation artist. He makes live, code-based artworks that run on a screen, in a projection, or across the walls of a room.

Each work grows from a single seed of code, which he calls Machine DNA: one seed, many bodies. The room is the art. Working in p5.js, WebGL, and GLSL shaders, he builds browser-based pieces that generate in real time and change with every viewing.

Practice

The work explores acceptance, control, and agency: what part of life can be controlled, and what has to be accepted. Series include Surrender Machines, Centered, Invisible Layer, Witness Mirror, Artificial Flowers, and Bloom.

He sells directly from the studio: pen-plotter drawings, digital pieces, and live installations for museums, lobbies, and private collections. Browse everything in the catalog.

Background

Walhimer began his career in the studios of Judy Pfaff (1985) and Donald Lipski (1987). He holds a BS in Fine Arts from Skidmore College (1985) and a Master of Industrial Design from Pratt Institute (1992).

He is also a museum planner. He is Managing Partner of Museum Planning LLC and the author of two books published by Bloomsbury: Museums 101 and Designing Museum Experiences. His institutional work spans three continents and informs an art practice concerned with spaces that respond to the people who inhabit them.

Prototyping board — rough layout

24" x 24" x 1/2" maple plywood · top edge faces visitors
Brain & network — connections

Ethernet feeds into the AMD7 mini PC (Linux), running openFrameworks, Pure Data, OSC, and DSP. The AMD7 connects to a 6-port switch (all 6 ports used). The switch links over WiFi to a noise machine used as a remote control, and fans out to six device nodes — LCD, touch + 5 buttons, LED panel (via its own Novastar), speakers x4 + DSP, projector (with projection mapping), and fans (cooling + presence relay) — each running on its own Teensy 4.1 or Raspberry Pi.

Notes

Hand-hold cutouts on the left and right edges (dashed) let the board be carried, leaned against something, or set on a shelf — not just used flat on a tabletop.

Top edge is the camera-facing edge — whatever side the camera mounts on becomes "the front," so it gets its own strip and a separate cable run.

Right-hand column is power & cooling, running the full height of the board — power strip, LED panel PSU, din rail, ultrasonic humidifier, and fans all sit on one edge so the noisiest, hottest, and wettest elements vent toward the back and stay clear of the signal/control side. The LED panel's own power supply is called out separately here so it doesn't get forgotten when wiring.

Left column is two zones — brain & network (AMD7, 6-port switch, Novastar, Teensy 4.1), and a combined display & controls zone covering everything the visitor experiences: LCD, touch, buttons, LED panel, speakers x4 + DSP, and projector + projection mapping.

The two diagrams above show how these connect: ethernet into the AMD7 (Linux, openFrameworks/Pd/OSC/DSP), down to the 6-port switch, with speakers wired directly off the AMD7 for audio, and a noise machine connected over WiFi as a remote control. The switch's other five ports each drive one device through its own Teensy 4.1 or Raspberry Pi — LCD, touch + buttons, LED panel (via Novastar), projector (with projection mapping), and fans (cooling + presence relay).

This is a starting arrangement — once you have real component footprints (AMD7 box dimensions, LCD size, etc.), the zones can become to-scale cutout outlines.

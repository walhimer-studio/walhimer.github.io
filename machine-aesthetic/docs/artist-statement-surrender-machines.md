# Artist Statement: Surrender Machines

**Mark Walhimer** — May 23, 2026

Full working draft (influence map, contacts): [artist-statement-surrender-machines.pdf](./artist-statement-surrender-machines.pdf)

---

I build **Surrender Machines**—autonomous digital entities with a lifespan. Digital media is usually valued for infinite repetition and flawless execution; I work in the opposite direction. Each machine is born with a unique **MachineDNA**: a seeded blueprint for its body, metabolism, and rate of decay. It is built to run down, not to loop forever.

The **kernel** of the work is not a decorative animation or a reactive pairing of sound and image. It is a virtual mechatronic system—gears, load, friction, failure—implemented in code (WebGL, Web Audio, openFrameworks, Pure Data). One mechanical state drives both what you see and what you hear. The sound is not a soundtrack laid over visuals; it is the audible friction of the machine’s internal metabolism—the meshing of teeth, the drag of wear, the choke of overload.

As the machine runs, it loses energy. Parts slip. Vertices drift. Oscillators stutter. I treat these **misfires** not as errors to hide but as the honest record of a system struggling inside time—generative logic meeting entropy. Visitors can load the mechanism further; they are co-authors of its stress, not an audience watching a finished composition.

Every Surrender Machine is designed to reach a final state. When its energy approaches zero, the frantic motion subsides into what I call a **Mechanical Sigh**: a low hum, a blurred field, the release of the desire to compute. The work is no longer a tool performing a task; it becomes a relic of its own existence. In that surrender I find a parallel to the Zen movement from desire to emptiness—and, in watching the machine let go, a small mirror of ourselves.
